## Case study 1: RiseTrack (habit tracker app)

**Portfolio title (Upwork, max 70 chars)**
Rejected iOS App Rescued and Approved on the App Store | Base44 + Capacitor

**Role:** iOS app developer and App Store submission

**Skills tags:** iOS Development, App Store Submission, Capacitor, React, Base44, Mobile App Development

**Project description**
The problem: RiseTrack, a French and English habit and task tracker built in Base44, was rejected by Apple after the previous developer's submission. The client could not launch.

What I did:
1. Found the root causes of the rejection and removed the risky parts (a non working Premium feature and extra permissions).
2. Replaced the old app shell with a clean Capacitor 7 wrapper.
3. Cut permissions down to only what the app needs (Camera and Photo Library).
4. Rebuilt, resubmitted, and handled all replies to Apple Review.
5. After approval, fixed habit carry over, duplicate logs and iPhone safe area layout.

Result: Apple approved RiseTrack 1.0 and it is live on the App Store. The client went from rejected to launched.

## Case study 2: FTLR Fitness (coaching app with Apple subscriptions)

**Portfolio title**
Apple In App Subscriptions Added to a Base44 Fitness App | Approved on App Store

**Role:** iOS developer, in app purchases, App Store submission

**Skills tags:** In App Purchases, RevenueCat, iOS Development, Capacitor, App Store Submission, Subscription Billing

**Project description**
The problem: A US fitness coach built her app in Base44, but Base44 cannot sell App Store subscriptions, and Apple rejects apps that sell digital subscriptions without Apple In App Purchase. Her first developer did not deliver, and her launch date was close.

What I did:
1. Built a Capacitor 8 iOS app with a native paywall, Restore Purchases and an "Already a member" path, so her existing web members keep access.
2. Set up RevenueCat and both subscription plans in App Store Connect (prices, screenshots, review notes).
3. Automated signing and uploads with Codemagic.
4. Created the app icon, splash screen and logo from her file.
5. Handled App Store review.

Result: Version 1.0 approved and live on the App Store. Subscriptions approved by Apple with version 1.0.1. The coach can now earn recurring revenue from iPhone users.

2. Plan selection and Restore Purchases screens
3. App Store listing
