# Proposal Structure (Adam's Upwork account)

How every Upwork proposal for Adam is built. Based on Adam's proposal framework (uploaded 23 Nov 2026), adapted to his account and his rules in `proposal-rules.md`.
If this file and `proposal-rules.md` disagree, `proposal-rules.md` wins (it holds Adam's own decisions).

---

## 1. Before writing

1. **Duplicate check.** Search `proposal-pipeline.md` and `list_freelancer_proposals` (list and invitations). Never apply twice to the same job or the same job text reposted.
2. **Known-buyer check.** Search `/projects/` and `proposal-pipeline.md` for the company, domain, contact or job text. Known buyer = no links, no business names, no cities. Proof becomes business type plus result.
3. **Research.**
   - Who is asking: owner, agency, or one-off.
   - Open their site, reviews and 1 or 2 competitors.
   - Note the job age. Early letters get read.
   - Open every link, sample file and screenshot in the post.
   - Run the quick site audit: phone load time, H1, meta description, tracking (GA4, Tag Manager, Meta Pixel), calls to action, booking or contact path.
4. **Find the fault.** One thing going wrong they have not named, or two symptoms they have not connected. No fault = the letter competes on credentials and loses. Never invent a fault. If you cannot check it, do not claim it.
5. **Core requirements.** List what the post says matters most ("key part of the role", must-haves, "please include your experience with..."). Each one gets a direct answer in the client's words.
6. **Trap check.** Look for codewords ("start with the word BLUE"), hidden instructions or AI traps. Follow a codeword. Skip jobs that are only there to trap AI letters.

## 2. The letter, in order

### Line one: the opening
Names a specific mechanism in their stack (setting, rule, file, plugin behaviour, platform limit) with a real number when there is one. Never restates the brief. No greeting. Four forms:

| Form | When |
|---|---|
| **Catch** | A checked mismatch inside the client's own materials. Beats every other form and goes first. |
| **Reframe** | Their framing misses the real cause. Use sparingly. |
| **Link** | Two symptoms they have not connected. |
| **Constraint** | They diagnosed it right, so name the trap in their plan. |

Codeword in the post? It goes first, on its own line.

### Sentence two: the cost
What it costs if missed, in their terms (lost calls, lost bookings, lost sales, ad money wasted).

### Reading between the lines
One short paragraph quoting a phrase from the brief and what it shows they care about. No flattery.

### What good looks like (one niche example)
Exactly one example site from `proposal-reference-sites.md` that matches the client's niche (closest niche if no exact match). Frame it as an example of what works, never as Adam's work:
> "The best [niche] sites, like https://[site], put [feature A], [feature B] and [feature C] up front. I would build yours around that same pattern."

Never write "I built", "my work" or "my client" about a reference site.

### The plan
"How I'd approach yours:" then a short numbered list, **backup first**. Or 2 to 4 short paragraphs on shorter letters. The client keeps ownership of their hosting, Search Console, GA4 and Tag Manager.

### Core requirements answered
Every must-have from step 1.5, answered plainly, with the closest real proof.

### Price line
See section 3.

### Live chat line
One line, no calls ever: "Happy to walk through this over live chat here on Upwork: 10 minutes on [agenda item 1], [agenda item 2] and access." No time or day.

### Closing question
One either/or question only someone who understood the job could ask. About the result, not process. Answerable in a word. No time or day. After a Catch, the decision the catch forces.
Example: "Should the booking button go in the header on every page, or only on the service pages?"

## 3. Price

1. **Sell one entry step**, not the whole job. The smallest piece with a visible result.
2. **Hourly:** bid Adam's rate ($25/hr, `proposal-rules.md` rule 12). Say "about X hours for the first step", 10 max, no dollar total. Also offer that first step as a small fixed milestone.
3. **Fixed:** the lower of the budget and the bid average, minus about 10%. Never over the budget. No budget or average: $300 or less.
4. **Cap:** no first letter over $500.
5. **Bigger work:** milestone 1 is the entry step. The rest is priced after milestone 1. No whole-job total.
6. **Precise figure:** $5 or $10 under the round number ($195, $340, $485).
7. **Expert or fixed-quote posts:** honest hours x real rate, no $500 cap. Over 3x their budget: don't bid, flag it to Adam.
8. **One free finding** in every letter: the fault from step 1.4, stated plainly. No free mockups or free work.
9. **Delivery speed:** 24 / 48 / 72 hours for small work, 4, 5 or 7 working days for bigger. Never longer than 7 working days per milestone.
10. **Service promise:** add the one line that matches the job, same wording as the portfolio site (Promises section). Never mix or stretch them.
    - New websites and redesigns: "14 days of free fixes after launch for anything I built."
    - WooCommerce stores: "Test orders placed before handover, and 14 days of free fixes on cart and checkout."
    - Speed optimization: "Before and after PageSpeed report. If the mobile score we agreed on is not reached, I keep working on it at no extra cost."
    - Bug fixes: "If the same bug comes back within 14 days, I fix it again at no extra cost."
    - Malware cleanup: "If the same infection comes back within 30 days, I clean it again at no extra cost."
    - Migrations: "Redirects mapped and checked, so your pages and Google rankings move with you."
11. **Absurd budgets** ($5 to $50 for a full website): don't bid.

Upsell later (Rule 3 in CLAUDE.md): once the first milestone is released, offer the next step as a package of about $300 or more, then a monthly care plan.

## 4. Screening answers

- Every question answered specifically, in full and truthfully.
- Spaced like the letter: short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences, blank line between, lists one item per line.
- Questions inside the post body with no separate fields are answered inside the letter.
- Never invent a result, a before/after number or a past project to answer a question. If Adam has no such example, say what he would do and cite the closest real site.

## 6. Hard rules

- **Length:** under 5,000 characters including the sign-off. Aim for 2,500 to 3,500. Short jobs (one fix, one page) can go down to about 1,200.
- **Banned words:** honest/honestly, upfront, transparency, delve, leverage, seamless, robust, comprehensive, tailored, streamline, unlock, showcase, cutting-edge, "It's not X, it's Y", "Let me know", "I'd be happy to", "feel free". No em dashes, no dashes as punctuation.
- **No payment words:** say "fund" and "release", never pay, payment, invoice, PayPal, Stripe, bank.
- **No outside contact:** no email, phone, WhatsApp or other channel.
- **No calls:** live chat on Upwork only.
- **Own numbers only:** never quote reviews, stars, job counts or results Adam does not have.
- **No gap lines:** never announce a gap in the record or comment on the profile.
- **Claim only hands-on skills:** WordPress, Elementor, WPBakery, WooCommerce, speed, fixes, maintenance, redesigns.
- **Timezone:** only from the "local time" on the post, never from the town name.

## 9. Proven shapes

**Standard:** named cause, a constraint they missed, short numbered plan backup first, links woven in, either/or close.

**Detail jobs:** Catch in line one with the URL, what it costs across the full job, a second smaller mismatch, one quoted line from the brief, plan that follows their own steps and field names, two proofs matched to the fault, every confirmation they asked for in their wording, either/or question from the catch. On these jobs the catch is the credential.

Log what wins and what gets ignored in `upsell-learnings.md` so these shapes keep improving.
